The Anatomy of a Cut

Five parts. One decision, made fifty-eight times.

  • TABLE

    The flat top facet

    The largest single facet — it's the window the eye looks through first, and the one most cutters are tempted to oversize for apparent carat weight.

  • CROWN

    The upper faceted slope

    Angled facets between the table and the girdle that break incoming light into the spectrum you actually see as "sparkle."

  • GIRDLE

    The stone's widest edge

    The thin band that meets the setting — where a lapidary's restraint shows most, since too thin chips and too thick reads clumsy.

  • PAVILION

    The lower body

    Everything below the girdle. Its angles decide whether light exits back through the crown — or leaks out the bottom, dulling the stone.

  • CULET

    The base point

    Ideally invisible. A culet that's too large opens a small window straight through the stone, visible as a dark or washed-out spot.
